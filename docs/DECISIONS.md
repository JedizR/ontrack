## T1a

Gain pace and hybrid training, the engine and science half, 12 November 2026. Unattended; decided on the owner's behalf:
- **The faster gain pace ships provisional, with its label.** No source recommends gaining faster than 0.5 percent of body weight a week. S7 was read again: 0.25 to 0.5 for novice and intermediate lifters, nothing above, "underweight" not mentioned. A search for a rate for underweight adults without an eating disorder (WHO 1999 severe malnutrition manual, ESPEN 2017, ASPEN 2020 and BAPEN refeeding guidance, NHS advice, trials in constitutionally thin women) found end points and refeeding limits, never a weekly rate. Aragon and Schoenfeld 2020 is paywalled; Slater and others 2019 advise starting at about 360 to 480 kcal a day and set no larger surplus for anyone. Shipping it anyway, because every limit around it is checked: the surplus cap (20 percent, S7) binds first for most lean, seated people, so vector H delivers 0.61 percent a week, about 75 kcal a day more than the standard pace; the protein target and F10's strength recommendation are unchanged; and the check-in corrects the target from the weight actually gained. Not shipping it would leave the friends' request unmet with nothing safer to offer. `C.gainPaceFaster` has status `provisional`; T1b shows its label (Being checked) wherever the pace or its breakdown appears.
- **0.75 percent a week** is the middle of what the 15 percent surplus group of Helms and others 2023 (S20) aimed for, 0.7 to 0.8. That trial and Garthe and others 2013 found the extra gain was mostly fat, so F19's words ("more of it as fat") are verified even though the rate is not.
- **Who may choose it: BMI under 20, and not advanced lifters.** Forbes 2000 (S21): thin people put 60 to 70 percent of an overfeeding gain into lean tissue, obese people 30 to 40; Hall 2007 adds that the evidence for gain is thinner than for loss. No source gives a BMI cut. 20 keeps the hardgainer of vector A (BMI 19.6) inside and is the same low end of normal as `gentleOnlyBmi`; 18.5 would leave out most of the "skinny" people who asked. Advanced lifters already get the lean pace only (S7). The pace is never a default.
- **A chosen faster gain pace holds past BMI 20** (from the code review). The engine first clamped it at every recomputation, as it does the loss paces; a hardgainer reaches BMI 20 three or four weeks in, and the check-in then read the step down as "You are gaining faster than planned", which was false, or kept the pace on an on track week anyway. The BMI limit is a rule for who may choose the pace, not a PRD section 7 guardrail, so gain paces are now limited by experience only in `planFromBaseline`, and onboarding's `inputOf` applies the BMI limit (a pace no longer offered after an answer changes falls back to the default, as the Pace screen already showed). The 20 percent surplus cap still bounds it at any weight. Stepping down later is the user's choice, in Settings (M9). The loss paces keep their clamp: those are guardrails.
- **Until T1b, onboarding leaves it out** (`offeredPaces` in `draft.ts` filters it): it needs F19's words and the provisional label first. The engine offers it, so plans and check-ins already handle it.
- **Hybrid points are arrangement advice, not recommendations,** in their own list (`ProgramAnalysis.hybrid`), so the Train tab's top three are unchanged. They fire only on a day with both a strength or circuit session and a cardio session; walks and sport do not count, since the trials used running and cycling. A leg day is lifting with quads, hamstrings or glutes as a primary muscle; calves alone do not make one. The gap is 6 hours (S23), the more conservative of the two figures read (S24 found 3 hours spared explosive strength). S24 and S25 disagree on whether running or cycling matters; cycling on leg days is the conservative advice. Wilson's frequency and duration finding (S25) is recorded but not a check yet.
- **Both hybrids follow their own points:** lifting first, cycling on the full body days, and their lines say "hours later if you can" for the gap (the code review found the first wording, "a short ride after each session", contradicted it). A template has no times, so the gap stays the user's call.
- **The two hybrid templates reuse the lifting days of Full body 3 days and Upper lower 4 days,** so their muscle balance is already in range, and add cardio after the lifting to make exactly the WHO's 150 minutes (cardio at 6.8 METs counts double). Full body and cardio 3 days rides after each session, every day being a leg day; Upper lower and cardio 5 days runs after the upper days only and adds a long easy ride on Saturday. Both show their arrangement points by design.
- **"What do you train?"** (owner's decision in the onboarding research, section 11) maps to sessions by `sessionsFor(kind, days)`: lifting an hour, cardio half an hour, both taking turns with lifting first, so a two-day "both" week has one of each. `suggestTemplate` follows the research's section 5 table, with Both going to a hybrid from three days: Upper lower and cardio 5 days from five days and a year of lifting, otherwise Full body and cardio 3 days. Both on one or two days gets Minimal 2 days, as lifting does: no hybrid fits two days.
- **The training card counts a session as done only when marked done;** an unmarked one is planned and not done. Cardio minutes are moderate-equivalent, as in the program analysis, so the card and Train agree.
- **Two catalog lines added** (`t.train.suits` for the hybrids) so the Templates sheet, which lists every template, has no empty line before T1b, and S20 to S25 in `src/lib/data/sources.ts` for the points' Why. No Svelte file changed.

## N3

Thai nutrition from Thai sources, 12 November 2026. The owner's calls: Ontrack is a class proof of concept that will not launch publicly, the interface stays English, and Thai script is for search aliases only. Decided on his behalf:
- **Why a Thai table may be used now.** N1 left the INMU Thai Food Composition Database out because its terms (online version 3, read 12 November 2026) let non-commercial users "access and use the data" but send "applications for permission to reproduce, distribute, or otherwise utilise materials" to INMU. Ontrack is now a private class project shown to a few people, never published, and the owner chose the closest Thai numbers over that wait. The values stay cited to INMU under every food. If Ontrack is ever made public, the INMU request in `docs/BLOCKERS.md` (N1) comes first, or the Thai rows go.
- **The owner's file** (`data/thai/owner/nutrition_dataset.csv`, 1,005 rows, SHA-256 `42703598...ca80`, provenance in `SOURCE.md` beside it) is an extract of the INMU database: its codes and names match. The build reads it as `owner:<code>` and fails if the file changes. Search cites it as its `SOURCE.md` asks: "Thai Food Composition Database, INMU, Mahidol University, via an extract the owner supplied", with a link to INMU's terms.
- **INMU's own pages are the Thai web source.** Each food's page in the online database (`inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd`) gives energy, protein, fat, carbohydrate and fibre per 100 g edible portion. The page's answer is kept verbatim in `data/thai/inmu/<code>.html`, with the page URL, the date read and a checksum in `data/thai/pages.json`; the build fetches a listed page it lacks (one a second) and fails on an edited one, as it does for SG FoodID. Ids are `inmu:<code>`. Search's portion step links to the food's page ("Source page").
- **Searched in Thai and not used** (WebSearch, 12 November 2026; "พลังงาน ต่อ 100 กรัม หมูปิ้ง", "ตารางคุณค่าทางโภชนาการอาหารไทย ลาบหมู น้ำตกหมู กรมอนามัย", "แคลอรี่ อาหารจานเดียว กรมอนามัย", "ลาบหมู ห่อหมก หมูปิ้ง คุณค่าทางโภชนาการ ต่อ 100 กรัม"): the Department of Health's Bureau of Nutrition describes a 2021 search system for its 2018 table (`nutrition2.anamai.moph.go.th/en/thaifcd/222012`) but no public search page answered, and the 2018 table is scanned images (N1). HDmall's nutrition hub (`hdmall.co.th/nutrition-hub`, a commercial health marketplace) republishes INMU's values, so INMU itself is cited instead. Calorie lists on Thai hospital, magazine and marketplace pages give calories per plate without protein, fat, carbohydrate or the plate's weight, which the build cannot turn into values per 100 g. No Thai university or hospital page with full values for the dishes INMU lacks was found.
- **One source per food:** the owner's row when it gives energy and agrees with INMU's page (within 20 percent where both were read), otherwise INMU's page. Cooked forms where they exist: cooked rice (A11), steamed sticky rice (A23), cooked brown rice (A59), boiled egg (H18), grilled and fried meats and fish.
- **Derived values** follow Atwater (4, 4, 9 kcal per gram, `V.atwater`): energy from the macros when a row has carbohydrate but no energy, carbohydrate as (energy - 4p - 9f) / 4 when it has energy but no carbohydrate. `foods.json` marks them (`source.derived`). The owner's carbohydrate column is CHOCDF, total carbohydrate by difference, so energy derived from it counts fibre at 4 kcal a gram, a little high for fibrous foods. From INMU's pages: energy by calculation, or total energy where a page has only that; available carbohydrate, or total; `complete` checks the two against each other.
- **Rows left out** (18 of the owner's file, listed in `docs/EVAL-NOTES.md`): energy more than 25 percent or 40 kcal from 4p + 4c + 9f; derived carbohydrate below -2 g (energy under what protein and fat alone carry; above -2 g it is rounding in lean fish and counts as 0); and J36, a "whey protein powder" at 7.8 g protein in 100 g. Both thresholds are `C.tableKcalTolerance` and `C.derivedCarbsMin`, working assumptions. T290 (pumpkin curry with pork) is left out as well: its carbohydrate column holds 47, which INMU's page shows is the energy (47 kcal, 3.3 g carbohydrate); the page is used instead.
- **Existing foods now from the Thai source** (step 4; ids, names and portions kept): listed with every difference over 20 percent in `docs/EVAL-NOTES.md`. Foods INMU has only in another form, or not at all, keep their table: pad thai with chicken, shrimp or pork stays FNDDS (INMU has one pad thai, now the plain Pad thai), and pad kra pao pork and beef, boat noodles, duck egg noodle soup, mango sticky rice, the tom yum with seafood and the drinks stay SG FoodID; fried banana stays SG FoodID too, since INMU's deep fried banana (524 kcal in 100 g) is the crisp snack. Fruit, vegetables, herbs and sauces stay on USDA: matching Thai varieties one by one is left for later.
- **Aliases moved** to the new Thai foods: `gai yang` and ไก่ย่าง from grilled chicken breast (now อกไก่ย่าง), `kor moo yang` and คอหมูย่าง from roast pork shoulder (now สันคอหมู), `moo krob` and หมูกรอบ from pork belly, `pla tu` and ปลาทู from Atlantic mackerel (now ปลาซาบะ), `tom yum goong` and ต้มยำกุ้ง from creamy tom yum to tom yum kung.
- **Search ranks a whole match first** (from the code review): with 98 short new names, ข้าวต้ม found khao tom mat before plain rice soup, ก๋วยเตี๋ยว pad kee mao before rice noodles. A name or alias equal to the query now beats a prefix. Generic aliases that now name a Thai dish moved too: `moo daeng` from pork tenderloin, `tao huay` from silken tofu, `sweet and sour chicken` from sweet and sour pork, `shumai` from dumplings, `kiao nam` from wonton soup.
- Names, aliases and portions are drafted by Claude as in N1 (a street plate about 300 to 350 g, a noodle soup 450 g, a curry bowl 250 g); no value is typed.

## B1

Barcode scanning (PRD F22) and supplements set from the tub, decided on the owner's behalf:
- **Open Food Facts is read from the browser, not through a server route.** Its API allows 15 product reads a minute per IP address (read 12 November 2026); from the browser that is each phone's own allowance, while a Vercel function would share egress addresses with every other Vercel project. It answers CORS with `*` and allows an `X-User-Agent` header, which the app sets to `Ontrack/<version> (https://ontrack-black.vercel.app)` since a browser cannot set User-Agent (OFF asks for an app name and contact; the owner may add a contact email). No key, no function, nothing to pay. The cost is a CORS preflight per lookup. Its data is open under the ODbL: "Values from Open Food Facts, open data under the ODbL" with its terms under every product from it.
- **No shared server cache of lookups.** A class proof of concept makes a few lookups a day; each user's products are their own cache (synced, offline). Revisit if Ontrack goes public.
- **The decoder:** `barcode-detector` 3.2.3 (MIT, monthly releases, last 19 October 2026), a ponyfill on `zxing-wasm` 3.1.6 (MIT, zxing-cpp inside). The reader's WebAssembly is 947 KB (414 KB gzip); it loads only when the scanner opens, from the site instead of the package's default CDN, and the service worker caches it then (not precached for everyone). Chrome's own `BarcodeDetector` is used where it reads the four formats. Initial JavaScript is unchanged in kind: the ponyfill is a 43 KB lazy chunk.
- **Product ids** are the barcode as 13 digits (UPC-A with a leading 0, UPC-E expanded), as Open Food Facts stores them, or 8 for EAN-8. Typed 8 digits are taken as EAN-8.
- **A product is kept when it is logged or its label confirmed,** with the amount as the next start. Lookups the user does not log are not stored. Undo of a first log keeps the product (it is only a cache).
- **Drinks are measured in ml** where Open Food Facts or the label says so (the owner's call for milk after N2); 1 ml is counted as 1 g in the meal item's `grams`, as labels give values per 100 ml.
- **The label reader** goes through `/api/analyze` as `kind: 'label'` (same auth, quota, size limit, timeout and retry) with its own prompt and schema; it never estimates. Calories from kilojoules use 4.184 (FAO FNP 77 section 3.1, S19, added to `constants.ts` and `docs/SOURCES.md`). The Label step asks the user to confirm per serving, like Correct and the supplement label; a label printing only per 100 g starts at a 100 g serving. Kept as read it is Label; any value typed or changed makes it Yours.
- **A label photo shows the photo notice first**, as the first meal photo does, with its own words ("Your photo of the label is sent to Google's Gemini to read its numbers, then discarded. Ontrack keeps only the numbers."); one consent covers both.
- **Typing the number** shows "Find product" right under the field, not pinned: the iPhone's number pad has no return key and a pinned button would sit under it.
- **Supplements lead with the tub** (owner, during B1): Kind, then From your tub with Scan the tub (violet) and Photograph the label, then a plain "Use typical values for now" (whey and plant only) or "Type the numbers". A scan or label fills the scoop as the serving over the scoops in it ("2 scoops (60 g)" in Open Food Facts' serving text, or the label reader's scoops per serving) and lets the values follow the scoop. Setting a supplement from its tub keeps the user's name for it; a new one takes the product's name. A supplement on typical values is labelled Typical on its amount step, its Add row, Today's entry caption and the logged item, with one plain "Set it from your tub" on the amount step.
- **Not done:** the light (torch) is offered only where the camera reports it; Safari may not. A product scanned in the supplement setup is not also kept as a product. "Use default values" does not exist for products: Correct the numbers again instead.
- Sheet titles now wrap to a second line instead of cutting off, for long product names (Search's food names benefit too).

## N1

Thai sources (step 1). Checked 12 November 2026; terms quoted in the pull request:
- **INMU Thai Food Composition Database**, online version 3 (August 2025), `inmu.mahidol.ac.th/thaifcd`: 313 mixed dishes, Thai and English names, per 100 g. Not used. Its terms authorise non-commercial users "to access and use the data", but "applications for permission to reproduce, distribute, or otherwise utilise materials" go to INMU by email; shipping the values inside an app is reproducing and distributing them. It also offers no file a build could read (search pages only). Recorded as a blocker with the exact request (`docs/BLOCKERS.md`).
- **Department of Health, Bureau of Nutrition, "Food Composition Table of Thai Foods"** (2018, 234 dishes; 2001 edition too): not used. The PDFs are scanned images with no text layer, so a build could read them only through OCR and a hand check of every value, which breaks "nobody types a nutrient value"; the department's website policy forbids distributing or deriving from its content without express permission, and the Thai Copyright Act B.E. 2537 section 14 makes government units the owners of works made in their employ (section 7 exempts laws, orders and official reports, not this table).
- **ASEAN Food Composition Database**, electronic version 1 (2014, INMU): 29 Thai dishes in a PDF with a text layer. Not used: the same INMU terms as the Thai database.
- **Used: SG FoodID** (Singapore Food Insights Database, Health Promotion Board, Singapore). Its data licence (terms section 10) grants a "worldwide, perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive licence to use, access, download, copy, distribute, transmit, modify, and adapt the Data ... whether commercially or non-commercially", with "a conspicuous notice acknowledging the source of the datasets and including a link to the most recent version of this Licence" (section 11) and nothing that suggests HPB endorses Ontrack. About 20 Thai dishes, mostly laboratory analysis (2010 to 2026), plus the Thai-Chinese dishes Thailand eats daily. The notice is the citation line in Search's portion step, with the date read and a link to the terms.
- **Used: USDA FNDDS 2021 to 2023** (public domain): pad thai with chicken, shrimp and meat, fried rice with chicken, pork, beef and shrimp, coconut rice. FNDDS has no tom yum, curries, som tam, larb or Thai desserts.
- PRD F14 says "a published Thai food composition source". No Thai source may be used yet, so N1 ships a Thai food table from published non-Thai sources (SG FoodID and FNDDS) and records the Thai one as a blocker.

The table:
- 571 foods, 138 tagged `thai` with Thai script aliases: 51 SG FoodID dishes, 8 FNDDS dishes, and 79 SR Legacy foods already in the table that Thai people eat (rice, noodles, eggs, pork cuts, fruit, herbs, sauces, coconut milk) given Thai script aliases. Thai-Chinese dishes (khao man kai, khao mu daeng, ba mi kiao, jok, kuay jap, rad na) count as Thai: they are everyday Thai street food under Thai names, and the Singapore dish is the same dish.
- Names are the common romanised Thai name where menus in Thailand use it (Pad kra pao pork with rice and fried egg, Khao man kai, Kuay jap), otherwise plain English (Green curry with chicken, Rice porridge with pork); both kinds of name, and Thai script, are aliases. ## M2

Science check (verdicts in `docs/SOURCES.md`):

- Moderate walking is 3.8 MET, not 3.5 (code 17190); light and hard walking are codes 17152 and 17200. Why: the Compendium's level walking codes for slow, moderate and brisk pace.
- Sport stays 4.0, 6.0, 8.0 MET as a working assumption; its stated basis changed. Why: they are not band mid points and the Compendium has no general sport code.
- Low weekly volume is under 5 sets (was under 6); beginners are told 5 to 9 is a fine start. Why: S11's categories are under 5, 5 to 9 and 10 or more.
- The 20 set ceiling stays, labelled an assumption. Why: S11 found too little data above 12 sets to set a limit.
- Advanced lifters gaining are offered the lean pace only, and their surplus cap is 10 percent. Why: S7 gives about 0.25 percent a week and a 5 to 10 percent surplus for advanced lifters.
- 7,700 kcal per kg stays, labelled a working assumption. Why: S6 says it overstates the energy per kg for leaner people, but a body fat dependent model is out of scope; the caps bound the deficit and the check-in corrects from measured weight.
- Movement factors 1.20 to 1.60 stay, labelled a working assumption. Why: FAO/WHO/UNU's 1.40 to 2.40 includes exercise, which Ontrack adds per session; its sedentary office example is 1.53, so the seated start may be low and the check-in raises it.
- Floors 1,200, 1,500 and 1,350 stay, labelled a working assumption. Why: S14 gives low calorie diet ranges, not floors; these sit at or above its lower bounds and the PRD guardrail sets them.
- S2 cites Cunningham 1991 only. Why: it states 370 + 21.6 x FFM; no Katch and McArdle primary source was found.
- The certainty stance no longer says "within about 10 percent for most people". Why: S18 says Mifflin-St Jeor was the most reliable equation, not that equations are within 10 percent for most.
- The 4, 4, 9 kcal per gram factors got a source, S19 (FAO 2003). Why: macros use them and every number needs one.
- BMI 16 for the doctor card is verified through a WHO EMRO guide citing WHO TRS 854. Why: TRS 854 itself could not be opened.

Engine choices where the spec was silent or self contradictory:

- Protein reference weight is body weight capped at the weight for BMI 30, instead of switching to the weight at BMI 25 above BMI 30. Why: the switch drops protein by about a sixth at BMI 30 and breaks the gate's "protein never falls when weight rises"; vector C is unchanged.
- Aerobic minutes count cardio, walk and sport sessions at 3 METs or more, double at 6 or more, instead of "cardio, hard counts double". Why: S13 defines moderate and vigorous in METs and counts walking; moderate cardio (6.8) is vigorous by that definition.
- Circuit sessions count as strength sessions. Why: S13's muscle-strengthening activity includes resistance circuits.
- A session counts toward a muscle's frequency when the muscle is a primary target of one of its exercises. Why: section 11 does not say; direct work is the conservative reading.
- The `floor` flag is set when the plan's lowest possible day (a rest or skipped day) falls under the floor. Why: matched mode and skips make any day a rest day, and the plan record has no calorie mode.
- The effective rate uses the even target after floors. Why: the matched week averages to it unless a floor lifts only some days.
- "Protein and fat exceed the target" is tested against the lowest day target. Why: fat is the same every day.
- A plan never runs faster than the paces offered at its BMI; a lose goal under BMI 18.5 gets no deficit. Why: a check-in recomputes at a new weight and must keep the guardrails.
- Check-in mean target is over every day in the window, mean intake over complete days. Why: vector F's 2,357.14 is the plan's weekly mean, which 12 complete days cannot produce.
- Check-in, rate only: the target rate is the plan's effective rate, and the change moves the baseline before the plan is recomputed. The proposal is the even target's actual move rounded to 10, in both paths. Why: what the user accepts should equal the number shown.
- A check-in move over 200 shifts the baseline back so the even target moves 200. Why: section 10 clamps the move, not the baseline.
- A check-in keeps the plan's exercise energy per weekday and its resting energy, and uses the trend weight for everything else. Why: section 10 recomputes sections 6 to 8 only.
- Weigh-ins span 14 days counting the first and last day. Why: the spec does not say; inclusive matches "two weeks of weigh-ins".
- The engine takes day numbers, not date strings, for weigh-ins and check-in days. Why: `dates.ts` is the only place that touches `Date` and the engine imports nothing; M8 converts.
- Trend smoothing and the slope live in `engine/trend.ts`, not `model/trend.ts`. Why: the check-in needs the slope and the engine imports nothing outside itself.
- Unit conversions (7, 60, 100) live in `constants.ts` as `U`. Why: the gate's check is simplest as "no literal but 0 and 1 in engine files".
- Exercise, template and template session names are data in `src/lib/data/`, not catalog strings. Why: like food names, they become the user's data once a program exists. Each template's "who it suits" line is M7's, in `en.ts`.
