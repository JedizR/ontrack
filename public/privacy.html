Privacy

Ontrack is a class project: a proof of concept of a calorie and training tracker, not launched to the public. This page says in plain words what it keeps, where it keeps it and who can see it.

The short version

  • Your log is yours. There are no ads, no selling of data and no analytics or tracking scripts.
  • Friends see only what you choose to share. Your weight, your targets and your food log are never shared.
  • Photos, labels and meal descriptions you analyse go to Google's Gemini service, which names the foods.
  • You can export your data, or delete your account and everything in it, from Settings.

What Ontrack keeps

  • Your account. Your email address. You sign in with Google or with a 6 digit code sent to your email, so there is no password. With Google, the sign in also passes on the name and picture on your Google account.
  • Your profile. Sex for the equation, birth year, height, body fat if you give it, how much you move, how long you have lifted, your units, your check-in day, and your answers to the health questions at the start.
  • Your plan. Your goal, pace and daily targets, and how they were worked out.
  • What you log. Meals and the foods in them, saved meals, weigh-ins, your training program and the sessions you marked done or skipped, check-ins, supplements, and products you scanned or typed in.
  • Meal photos. A small copy of each photo you log, 320 pixels wide, kept with its meal. The full photo is not kept.
  • Friends, if you use them. Your handle, display name, profile photo if you add one, your friend list, and which meals you shared.

Where it is kept

  • First on your phone, in your browser's own storage (IndexedDB), so Ontrack works without a connection.
  • Then it syncs to a database run by Supabase, in Singapore, so it is backed up and reaches your other devices. The database lets only you read your own records.
  • The app is hosted by Vercel. Its small server functions run in Singapore too.
  • Before you save your plan and sign in, your answers stay in the browser on that phone only.
  • Signing out sends anything not yet synced, then deletes your data from that phone.
  • Ontrack sets no cookies. Your sign in is kept in the browser's storage on your phone.

Photos, labels and meal descriptions

When you analyse a meal photo, a nutrition label or a meal you describe in words, Ontrack sends it through its own server (/api/analyze) to Google's Gemini API. Before a photo leaves your phone, it is shrunk to 1024 pixels and saved again as a new picture, which drops its location and the other details a camera stores in the file. The request carries no name, email or account details.

Ontrack's server does not save the photo or the words. Its logs note only how long the analysis took, which model ran and how many foods it found. What Ontrack keeps is the result, the foods and grams it found, with the meal, and the small 320 pixel copy of the photo.

The AI only names foods and estimates grams. Calories, protein, carbs and fat come from food tables whenever there is a match.

Each account can run up to 40 analyses a day, and the app as a whole has a daily cap, so the service stays within its free limits. The count starts again at midnight UTC, which is 7 in the morning in Thailand.

Ontrack uses Gemini's paid tier. Under Google's terms for paid services, Google does not use what is sent, or the answers, to improve its products; it keeps them for a limited time to detect abuse and meet legal duties. Even so, leave people, documents and anything private out of the photos you analyse.

Barcode lookups

When you scan a barcode, the camera picture is read on your phone and is not sent anywhere. Your phone then asks Open Food Facts for that product directly. The request holds the barcode number and the app's name, nothing about you, though like any website Open Food Facts can see your internet address.

Product details from Open Food Facts are open data under the Open Database License (ODbL), and Ontrack credits it wherever its data shows.

Friends and the food map

Nothing is shared until you turn it on.

  • Your profile for friends is a handle, a display name and an optional photo. Anyone signed in to Ontrack can find you by your handle and see these three things, so they can send you a request. Nobody signed out can. A profile photo is redrawn on your phone as a square before it is uploaded, which drops its location.
  • Friends. One of you sends a request, and the other accepts or declines it. Either of you can remove the friendship at any time.
  • Your training program shows to your friends only while its switch is on. It is off to begin with.
  • Meals on the map. Each photo has its own choice to share it to the map, off every time unless you turn it on. Your friends, and nobody else, see a shared meal's photo, title, calories, protein, carbs and fat, the time, and a rounded place.
  • Location. The place comes from the location saved in the photo, or from your phone's position, read once when you take the photo, after you turn sharing on. Ontrack never uses live location and never keeps a track. The place is rounded to about 100 m on your phone before it leaves it, on a fixed grid, so sharing from the same spot many times does not give the exact spot away. The map's background tiles load from OpenFreeMap, which sees the area you look at but none of your meals.
  • Taking it back. Turning sharing off for a meal, deleting the meal or removing a friend takes it off the map and out of that friend's reach at once. One limit: a photo a friend already opened can stay in their browser for up to an hour, and anyone you show a photo to could save a copy.
  • Never shared: your weight, targets, daily totals, food log, meals you did not share, other photos, streaks or rankings.

Export and delete

  • In Settings you can export all your data as a JSON file, and your meals as a CSV file.
  • Deleting a meal deletes its photo from storage too. Replacing your profile photo deletes the old one.
  • Delete account, in Settings, deletes everything on the server at once: first your profile, shared meals and shared program, so friends stop seeing them; then every photo; then every record; then the account itself. The app then signs you out and deletes your data from that phone. Ontrack keeps no copy.

What Ontrack does not do

  • No ads, and no selling, renting or trading of your data.
  • No analytics, tracking pixels or third party scripts. The app loads its code and fonts from its own address.
  • Vercel and Supabase keep the usual server logs, such as internet addresses and the times of requests, to run the service and keep it secure.

Age

Ontrack is for adults, 18 and over. If the birth year you give makes you younger, the setup ends there and nothing is saved to an account.
